\begin{abstract}
We replace the dyadic hazard-band argument in the OpenAI matroid prophet
construction by a one-step potential argument. It gives expected exit hazard
greater than $5/16$. Together with a sharper use of its residual encoding,
this yields a proposed universal guarantee greater than $2^{-43}$ in the
original one-sample, full-seed almighty-adversary model, with $44n+26$ fair
seed bits. The exact coefficient is approximately $1.04159\cdot2^{-43}$.
This is an AI-assisted draft with a written proof, exact scalar certificates,
and finite checks of the new conditional inequalities; independent review
and formalization are pending. The coefficient exceeds Daniel's announced
$2^{-47}$ headline, but that proof's assumptions and strongest derived
constant remain unavailable. We make no priority or best-known-bound claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and dependencies}

\begin{theorem}
For every known finite labeled matroid $M$ on $n$ elements, there is a
measurable online rule using one independent sample per element and
$44n+26$ initial fair bits, whose law does not depend on the value
distributions, with the following guarantee. Let all sample and value
coordinates $S_e,V_e$ be mutually independent and nonnegative, with
matching laws at each label; let the seed be independent of these
coordinates. Assuming only $\E\OPT_M(V)<\infty$, for every measurable
arrival permutation depending on all samples, values, and the full seed,
the rule accepts an independent set at every prefix and earns expected
reward at least $2^{-43}\E\OPT_M(V)$.
\end{theorem}

We use the density paths, reverse computation, hybrid safety comparison,
marked-pivot encoding, and order-uniform accounting of OpenAI's manuscript
\cite{source}. The earlier note \cite{previous} specifies the algorithm and
its affine branch mixing and finite-offset rounding. We prove the new
hazard estimate here and state every changed quantitative deduction.
Source combinatorial lemmas remain explicit dependencies. No polynomial-time
algorithm or bounded observation precision is asserted.

\section{One-step logarithmic resource}

Fix a pre-step reverse transcript while a nonloop $d$ is alive. The remaining
coordinates $V$ have their independent guard-bit laws. Let $\mathcal A$ be
the increasing event that this transition survives, and let
$p=\Prb(\mathcal A^c)>0$. The source transition queries coordinates
predictably, at most once, and its queried positive answers alone form a
certificate for $\mathcal A$ whenever the transition survives.

Let $V'$ be the remaining domain after the complete transition, and let
$q$ be the failure probability of $\mathcal A$ when only $V'$ is random
and every queried coordinate is reset to zero. Then $p\le q\le1$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local}
 \E\log(q/p)\ge1-p.
\end{equation}
To verify this, during the transaction write $W$ for the remaining domain,
$I$ for the queried positives, $f(W)$ for the failure probability with only
$W$ random, and $f_I(W)$ for the failure probability with $I$ forced positive.
The ratio $Z=f_I(W)/f(W)$ starts at one and belongs to $[0,1]$.
For the next predictably selected query, $W'=W\setminus\{e\}$, product
conditioning gives
\[
 \E[Z-Z'\mid\text{transcript}]
 =Z\left(1-\frac{f(W)}{f(W')}\right)
 \le\log f(W')-\log f(W).
\]
At survival the final ratio is zero; otherwise it is at most one.
Thus its expected net decrease is at least $1-p$. Sum the display over
the finite query sequence to obtain \eqref{eq:local}. A stopped sequence
can be padded by zero increments. This is the source log-resource
calculation applied to one transaction, without its later hazard-band loss.

\section{The next hazard and a telescoping potential}

Write the current reverse map as $\mathcal G_k(c,S)$ and its output as $S'$.
The source query domains are precisely the departing coordinates. The
remaining domain $V'$ is the union of the group restrictions to $S'$.
Conditional on the complete post-step transcript these bits retain their
product law. On survival let $r$ be the next conditional exit probability.
For every assignment $c$ supported on $V'$, monotonicity in time and state
gives
\[
 \mathcal G_{k-1}(c,S')\subseteq\mathcal G_k(c,S).
\]
The right side resets queried bits to zero, just as the definition of $q$
requires. Therefore the next survival event is contained in the depleted
current survival event, so $r\ge q$ on survival. Both input states are
frozen in this comparison; earlier states are not recomputed after resetting
bits. No arrival order is conditioned upon.

Since exit has probability $p$ and $\log(q/p)\le\log(1/p)$ on exit,
\eqref{eq:local} yields
\[
 \E[\one_{\rm survive}\log(r/p)]\ge1-p-p\log(1/p).
\]
Use $\log(r/p)\le(r-p)/p$ to conclude
\begin{equation}\label{eq:drift}
 \E[\one_{\rm survive}r]-p
 \ge p-p^2(2+\log(1/p)).
\end{equation}
At $p=0$ the right side is interpreted as zero and the inequality is
immediate; at $p=1$ it is equality.

Define a potential equal to the current conditional hazard while $d$ is
alive and zero after exit. Every nonloop exits within the deterministic
finite reverse horizon. Summing \eqref{eq:drift} gives, if $P$ is the hazard
at the actual exit and $p_{\rm start}$ the initial hazard,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:global}
 \E[P(2+\log(1/P))]\ge1+p_{\rm start}\ge1.
\end{equation}
Here $\sum\E[\one_{\rm alive}p]=1$, and weighting any function of $p$
by the exit indicator is equivalent in expectation to multiplying by
$\one_{\rm alive}p$. Exits at zero hazard have probability zero.

The function $f(x)=x(2-\log x)$, with $f(0)=0$, is increasing and
concave on $[0,1]$. Jensen gives $f(\E P)\ge\E f(P)\ge1$.
Furthermore
\[
 e^{6/5}>\sum_{j=0}^4\frac{(6/5)^j}{j!}
 =\frac{2059}{625}>\frac{16}{5},
\]
so $f(5/16)<(5/16)(2+6/5)=1$. We have proved
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:hazard}
Conditional on any fixed filter and density masks, every nonloop in the
source reverse process has expected exit hazard strictly greater than
$5/16$. Equivalently, its total expected squared-exit mass is greater
than $5/16$.
\end{lemma}

The source hybrid comparison turns squared-exit mass into pre-parity
safety. Subtract the baseline-birth rank loss $|D_h|/\kappa$, average
fair parity, and then average the independent quarter-rate density mask.
The resulting safe-count coefficient is
\[
 \E[\sigma_i\mid H]\ge\gamma|Y_i|-\frac{n_i}{8\kappa},
 \qquad\gamma=\frac{5}{128}.
\]

\section{Sharper residual count and tail}

For $q=\lfloor n/\kappa\rfloor$ and
$S_d(n)=\sum_{j=0}^d\binom nj$, the first marked sequence in the
source residual encoding has only $q$ movable density generators;
the second has at most $2q$ generator and witness occurrences.
Consequently its existing marked-pivot lemma gives
\[
 |\mathfrak R_i|\le S_q(n)^2(4^qS_q(n))(4^{2q}S_{2q}(n)).
\]
The standard binomial bounds used in the source imply
\[
 \log|\mathfrak R_i|\le\frac n\kappa
    (5\log\kappa+5+4\log2)
 \le\frac n\kappa(5a+12)\log2\quad(\kappa=2^a\ge4),
\]
using $\log2>2/3$. As in the source, this family is fixed conditional
on the filter, parity, and exterior masks, before any focal bits or
the focal listing event are exposed.

The probability of sacrifice at a focal coordinate is
$p_O=(1+3t)/4$. Retain it in the tail estimate. Since
$\log(1+3t)\le3t$ and $\log2>2/3$,
\[
 \log_2(1/p_O)\ge2-5t=:\ell.
\]
Choose
\[
 \kappa=2^{12},\quad t=2^{-20},\quad B=2^8,\quad
 \delta=\frac{84}{\ell\kappa}.
\]
The residual-family union bound for every $n\ge\kappa$ is at most
$2^{-12n/\kappa}\le2^{-12}$. Thus the nominal-rank estimate has
transfer error
\[
 \epsilon=\frac1{8\kappa}+\frac{84}{\ell\kappa}+2^{-12}.
\]
Unlisted groups and outcomes with nominal rank greater than $n/\kappa$
use the same deterministic branches as in the earlier derivation.

\section{Final coefficient and evidence}

Set $\beta=(B-1)/B$ and mix the main branch with probability
$p=1-t/4=1-2^{-22}$. The order-uniform accounting from the earlier note
gives the slack
\[
 s=\gamma/2-\epsilon-t\kappa-\frac{t\kappa+1/4}{B-1}
 =\frac{4790554237}{1168228319232}.
\]
The maximum branch cancels the negative largest-coordinate term because
$(1-p)/4=t/16\ge pt\gamma$. Average eight shared phases
$J=0,\ldots,7$, all visible to the adversary, to obtain
\[
 C=\frac{p\beta^2ts}{8\kappa}
 =\frac{25618620910087559025}{216345186630601448946989472415744}
 >2^{-43}.
\]
For an elementary check, $p\beta^2>127/128$ and
$s>(128/127)/256$, while $t/\kappa=2^{-32}$.
The minimum over orders stays inside the seed expectation, and the
source glued-vector simulation transfers the coefficient losslessly
to the prophet model. Predrawing the masks costs $44n$ bits, and the
phase, branch, and parity require $3+22+1=26$ more bits.

Exact rational code checks all displayed constants. Finite reverse-process
checks cover 170 matroid/weight instances, 4,916 conditional drift
inequalities, and 3,153 global exit-hazard inequalities. Logarithms in
those checks use rational intervals from an atanh series after dyadic
reduction. The checks include the comparison $r\ge q$, positive
certificates, and a negative control demonstrating that monotone hazards
alone are insufficient. These finite checks support the written proof;
they do not prove its universal assertion. Same-agent second-pass
verification is not independent review.

The coefficient is about $16.6655$ times the announced $2^{-47}$ headline
\cite{announcement}. The unavailable announcement's exact model and
strongest derived coefficient remain unresolved. The most important new
review target is the depleted-event comparison used in \eqref{eq:drift}.
The earlier scalar-template limitation still holds for its old exit
coefficient; Lemma~\ref{lem:hazard} changes that ingredient.
