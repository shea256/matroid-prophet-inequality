\begin{abstract}
We refine the analysis of OpenAI's one-sample matroid prophet construction
against an adversary seeing all samples, values, and the entire random seed.
A joint affine representation of two residual pivot tests reduces their
combined counting dimension. A density-sample cutoff further reduces the
residual family, with exceptional outcomes explicitly charged. Retaining the
quadratic exit-hazard bound and recalibrating masks and finite phases gives
a proposed guarantee above $2^{-34}$ with $37n+24$ initial fair bits.
A compact exact coefficient is $1.055322895\ldots\,2^{-34}$, more than four
times the preceding published certificate. This AI-assisted work has a written
proof and same-agent verification. Independent review and formalization are
pending; no priority or strongest-known-bound claim is made.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and dependencies}
\begin{theorem}
For each known finite labeled matroid $M$ on $n$ elements there is a measurable,
distribution-independent online rule using one sample per label and $37n+24$
initial independent fair bits with the following property. Let all nonnegative
sample/value coordinates $S_e,V_e$ be mutually independent, with matching laws
at each label and an independent seed. If $\E\OPT_M(V)<\infty$, then for every
measurable arrival permutation depending on all samples, values, and the entire
seed, the rule maintains independence at every prefix and earns expectation
at least $C_1\E\OPT_M(V)$, where
\[
 C_1=\frac{14995972860899}{244123247301151948800000}>\frac{21}{20}\,2^{-34}.
\]
\end{theorem}

We use the density maps, guarded paths, hybrid safety comparison, marked-pivot
encoding, rank accounting, and lossless simulation in OpenAI's source
\cite{source}, pinned at commit \texttt{fd4aeeb2ee4f} (full revision in the linked source).
We specify the rule and source interfaces here, prove the new quadratic
transaction and exit bounds, give the joint residual encoding and density
cutoff, and derive the refined final constants. The structural matroid lemmas remain cited dependencies.
No polynomial-time or bounded-precision claim is made.

\section{The fixed-vector rule and its statistics}
Fix a nonnegative hidden vector $w$ before any seed bits. The rule reveals
and sacrifices a seed-chosen mask and must handle every permutation; its
target is $\E_R\min_\pi w(A(w,R,\pi))$. Write
$\rk(A\mid P)=\rk(A\cup P)-\rk(P)$ and $F_{\varnothing}=\cl(\varnothing)$.
Fix a label order for every tie. Higher rounded weight means higher priority.
Put $W=\OPT_M(u)$ and let $I^*$ be priority greedy on the full rounded vector.

Use the parameters derived below:
\[
 \kappa=640,\quad t=2^{-15},\quad \Prb(H_e)=3/4,\quad
 \Prb(D_e)=7/16,\quad\Prb(C_e)=\Prb(T_e)=t,
\]
with main probability $1-t$, fair parity, and uniform shared phase
$J\in\{0,\ldots,255\}$. Put $\theta=261/256$, $B=\theta^{256}$, and
$u_J(x)=\theta^JB^{\lfloor\log_B(x/\theta^J)\rfloor}$ for $x>0$,
with $u_J(0)=0$. All coins are independent and drawn initially.

The maximum branch reveals a separate fair mask and accepts the first
unmasked positive nonloop with priority above every revealed positive nonloop.
An empty threshold is below every positive nonloop. If $m_*$ is the largest
rounded nonloop weight, expected worst-order reward is at least $m_*/4$:
the largest-priority nonloop is unmasked and the second is masked with
probability $1/4$. With only one positive nonloop success has probability
$1/2$; with none the bound is zero.

In the main branch reveal and sacrifice $H\cup D\cup C$. Define
\[
 U=\{e\notin H:u_e>0,\ e\notin\cl(H_{>e})\},\qquad Y=I^*\setminus H.
\]
The candidate test uses only revealed $H$ values and the tested value.
For a fixed phase divide weights by $\theta^J$, so true groups $U_i$ have
weight $B^i$. List those meeting $D$ in increasing weight order as
$G_1,\ldots,G_m$; put $D_j=D\cap G_j$, and similarly $C_j,T_j$.
The full groups are analysis objects; their revealed $D_j,C_j$ parts suffice
for preparation. If there are no listed groups, reject all.

For a flat $P$, let $Q_j(P)$ be the largest flat $Z\supseteq P$ maximizing
$|D_j\cap Z|-\kappa\rk(Z)$. Take closure first for nonflat arguments.
Set $a_j=-3j$ and enable $Q_{j,k}=Q_j$ for $k\ge a_j$, with the identity
earlier. Starting from $F_0(k)=F_{\varnothing}$ for $k<0$ and $E$ otherwise, define
\[
 X_j(k)=Q_{j,k}(F_{j-1}(k)),\qquad
 F_j(k)=\cl(X_j(k)\cup(C_j\cap X_j(k+1))).
\]
Compute paths in increasing $j$ on the finite relevant auxiliary timeline,
and put $\widehat F=F_m$. For a candidate nonloop in listed group $j$,
write $b_j(e)=\min\{k:e\in X_j(k)\}$. It is eligible precisely when it is
unsacrificed, $T$-positive, has birth $b\ge a_j+2$ of the chosen parity,
and lies outside $\widehat F(b-2)$. At endpoint $b$ maintain a set $A_b$;
accept an eligible $e$ exactly when $\rk(\{e\}\mid\widehat F(b-2)\cup A_b)=1$.

The source density and path lemmas give
\[
 F_{j-1}(k)\subseteq X_j(k)\subseteq F_j(k)\subseteq X_j(k+1),\qquad
 |D_j\cap(\cl(Q_j(P)\cup Z)\setminus Q_j(P))|\le\kappa\rk(Z\mid Q_j(P)).
\]
Nonloops are born at $a_j$ or in $[a_j+2,0]$, and the entire nominal path
uses a generator set of size at most $|D_j|/\kappa$. The nested layer bases
give prefix independence. Finite rank operations, fixed tie rules, and
countably many rounding levels give measurability. These structural facts
are inherited from the cited source.

For valid selected-parity endpoints $a_j+2\le b\le0$, define
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 K_{j,b}&=\bigcup_{\ell<j}(T_\ell\cap(X_\ell(b)\setminus X_\ell(b-1))),\\
 P_{j,b}&=G_j\cap(X_j(b)\setminus X_j(b-1)),\quad
 O=D\cup C,\quad S_{j,b}=P_{j,b}\setminus O,\\
 z_j&=\sum_b\rk(S_{j,b}\mid X_j(b-2)\cup K_{j,b}),\\
 \lambda_j&=\sum_b\rk(T_j\cap S_{j,b}\mid\widehat F(b-2)\cup K_{j,b}).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The safe count $\sigma_j$ counts $d\in D_j\cap Y$ with such a birth and
$d\notin Q_j(F_{j-1}(b-2)\cup K_{j,b})$. For each nominal summand choose
a fixed-order basis extension $Z_b$, so $\sum_b|Z_b|=z_j$. The residual is
\[
 \mathcal R_j=\bigcup_b\left(P_{j,b}\setminus
 \cl(X_j(b-2)\cup K_{j,b}\cup Z_b)\right)\subseteq O.
\]
Extend $\sigma_i,z_i,\lambda_i$ by zero on unlisted true groups.
The source density argument gives $\sigma_j\le\kappa z_j+|\mathcal R_j|$.
Its order-uniform lemma bounds the number of accepts of weight at least that
of $G_j$ below by $\lambda_j$, for every seed and order. One later-path
generator budget over the whole timeline is
\[
 c_j=|C_j|+\sum_{\ell>j}(|C_\ell|+|D_\ell|/\kappa).
\]
Conditional on $H,D,C$, parity, and test bits outside the focal group,
independent test thinning gives $\E[\lambda_j\mid\mathcal F_{-j}]
\ge t(z_j-c_j)$. These are the source interfaces used below.

\section{A quadratic transaction inequality}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:transaction}
Let $\mathcal A$ be an increasing event on finitely many independent bits,
and let a predictable query procedure, reading each bit at most once,
determine whether $\mathcal A$ occurs. Write $p=\Prb(\mathcal A^c)$. At the
completed transcript let $q$ be the failure probability with only unqueried
bits random and every queried bit reset to zero. Then
\[
 \E[\one_{\mathcal A}q^2]\ge p(1-p).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At an intermediate transcript, let $W$ be its remaining coordinates and $I$
its queried positives. Let $m$ be the actual conditional failure probability,
and $q$ the failure probability with only $W$ random and all other bits zero.
Predictable product exposure identifies $m$ with the probability when $I$ is
forced positive and $W$ is random. Increasingness gives $0\le m\le q\le1$.
Initially $m=q=p$.

For a predictably chosen next coordinate, the depleted probability $q'$ is
determined before the answer and satisfies $q'\ge q$. The actual conditional
failure probability is a martingale: $\E[m'\mid\text{transcript}]=m$.
Therefore the potential $\Phi(q,m)=q^2-2qm$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:query}
 \E[\Phi(q',m')\mid\text{transcript}]-\Phi(q,m)
 =(q'-q)(q'+q-2m)\ge0.
\end{equation}
At the end $m=\one_{\mathcal A^c}$, and failure forces $q=1$.
The initial potential is $-p^2$; its final expectation is
$\E[\one_{\mathcal A}q^2]-p$. Sum \eqref{eq:query} over the finite number
of queries, padding stopped paths by zeros, to obtain the result.
The proof includes $p=0,1$ and transactions needing no queries.
\end{proof}

\section{The reverse exit hazard}
Fix the source's filter and density masks, a listed-group prefix, and a
nonloop $d$ still alive before a reverse step. Its current states are $S$,
and its transition is $\mathcal G_k(c,S)$. The remaining guard coordinates
have their product law. Let $\mathcal A$ be the increasing event that $d$
survives, and let $p$ be the conditional exit probability.

For completeness, for $k<0$ the source reverse map on a listed-group
prefix is defined recursively by
\[
 A_0=F_{\varnothing},\qquad N_j=Q_{j,k}(A_{j-1}),\qquad
 A_j=\cl(N_j\cup\{e\in G_j\cap S_j:c_e=1\}),
\]
with output $(N_j)_j$. Starting from $X_j(0)=E$, reverse steps compute
$X(k)=\mathcal G_k(C,X(k+1))$. For actual later states, $N_j\subseteq S_j$
for every fresh bit assignment. After computing $N_j$, query only
$G_j\cap(S_j\setminus N_j)$; omitted additions already lie in $N_j$.
The new remaining domain is $\bigcup_j(G_j\cap N_j)$.

The source queries exactly the departing coordinates in predictable batches.
Their answers determine all output states: unqueried bits are inside flats
already spanned. Thus the transcript decides $\mathcal A$, on both survival
and failure, so Lemma~\ref{lem:transaction} applies.

Let $S'$ be the actual output and $V'$ the remaining coordinates. For each
assignment $c$ supported on $V'$, monotonicity in time and state gives
\[
 \mathcal G_{k-1}(c,S')\subseteq\mathcal G_k(c,S).
\]
The right side has all just-queried coordinates zero, exactly the depleted
experiment defining $q$. Conditional on the full post-step transcript,
the bits in $V'$ remain independent. On survival the next hazard $r$ therefore
satisfies $r\ge q$. The states are frozen in this comparison; earlier
states are not recomputed after resetting bits. No order is conditioned on.

The next alive squared hazard consequently obeys
\[
 \E[\one_{\rm survive}r^2]-p^2\ge p-2p^2.
\]
Telescope this potential over the deterministic finite reverse horizon.
Every nonloop exits, and if $P$ is its hazard at actual exit, then
\[
 -p_{\rm start}^2\ge
 \sum_i\E[\one_{\rm alive}p_i]-2\sum_i\E[\one_{\rm alive}p_i^2]
 =1-2\E P.
\]
Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hazard}
 \boxed{\E P\ge(1+p_{\rm start}^2)/2\ge1/2.}
\end{equation}
In particular, the source's total squared-exit mass is at least $1/2$.
Its hybrid comparison still applies because guard and test rates agree.
The same baseline-birth loss and fair parity give, after averaging a density
mask of arbitrary rate $\rho$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:safe}
 \E[\sigma_i\mid H]\ge \gamma|Y_i|-\frac{\rho n_i}{2\kappa},
 \qquad\gamma=\rho/4.
\end{equation}
This averages before conditioning on listing, extending the statistics by
zero on unlisted groups.

The bound $1/2$ is sharp for the abstract hypotheses. Take $N$ Bernoulli-$t$
bits and repeatedly test the OR of the remaining suffix, querying until the
first positive or until that suffix is exhausted. The next hazard equals
the depleted current failure probability. With $a=1-t$, classification by
the last positive (or none) gives
\[
 \E P=a^{2N}+t\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}a^{2j}
 =a^{2N}+\frac{1-a^{2N}}{2-t}.
\]
For $t=1/m$, $N=m^2$, this tends to $1/2$. This is an abstract limitation,
not a hard matroid instance or a bound on the best prophet algorithm.

\section{Joint residual encoding and a density cutoff}
Fix $H$, parity, and all mask bits outside a true group $U$ of size
$n\ge\kappa$. Do not condition on its focal bits or listing. Its incoming
path $F(k)$, competition sets $K_b$, index, and activation $a$ are fixed.
Write $S_d(N)=\sum_{i=0}^d\binom Ni$. Suppose a path has density generators
$J$ and rank witnesses $Z$, of sizes $j,z$. A generator has insertion time
$\tau(g)\in[a,0]$, and
\[
 X(k)=\cl(F(k)\cup\{g\in J:\tau(g)\le k\}).
\]
The source gives two marked streams. First, list $F(k)$ and then generators
inserted at $k$, marking the block one exactly when $k$ is a valid endpoint
of the chosen parity. Second, start with the fixed $F(b_0)$ marked zero,
where $b_0\in\{a-2,a-1\}$ has that parity. At each subsequent parity endpoint
$b$, insert $K_b,Z_b$ with mark zero, then $F(b)$ and generators with
$b-2<\tau(g)\le b$ with mark one. End at $b_1\in\{0,1\}$ of that parity;
set $K_b=Z_b=\varnothing$ at invalid endpoints.

The second stream has intermediate span $\cl(X(b-2)\cup K_b\cup Z_b)$ and
endpoint span $X(b)$, since $K_b\subseteq F(b)$ and $Z_b\subseteq X(b)$.
The first test selects valid births. For a label passing it, the second
pivot lies in the same layer and has mark one exactly on the residual.
Thus the intersection of the two mark-one sets is the residual.

\begin{lemma}[Joint pivot count]
For fixed $J,Z$, the number of residuals obtained from these linked streams
is at most $3^j4^{2j+z}S_{j+z}(2n)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The source marked-pivot argument first bounds retained greedy bases of
occurrences by $4^s$ when $s$ specified movable occurrences are inserted into
an old sequence spanning the matroid. To recall why, old-only greedy gives
an ordered basis. Only at most $s$ of its positions can be omitted after
insertions: their conditional-rank drops for the full movable set sum to at
most $s$. There are at most $2^s$ choices of omitted old occurrences and
$2^s$ of retained movable occurrences. Apply this to the two streams with
$s=j$ and $s=j+z$, giving $4^{2j+z}$ pairs of retained bases. Fix a pair.
Each nonloop has a unique minimal support in each basis, by matroid
submodularity, and its pivot is the last support member.

We represent both streams by the same $j+z$ variables. Label the entire
ground set $0,\ldots,N-1$, put $m=N+1$, $T=8m$, and $u_k=(k-a+2)T$.
Give old $F(k)$ occurrences in the first stream absolute scores
$2^{u_k+4m+e}$, with sign equal to their fixed marks. In the second stream,
the initial $F(b_0)$ has negative scores $-2^{u_{b_0}+4m+e}$;
$K_b$ has scores $-2^{u_b+e}$; and $F(b)$ has scores $2^{u_b+4m+e}$.
These are fixed before the focal masks.

Give each generator a variable $x_g=2^{u_{\tau(g)}+6m+g}$. Its first score
is $\eta_gx_g$, positive exactly at a valid endpoint. Its second score is
$d_gx_g$, where $d_g=1$ for the chosen parity and $d_g=2^T$ otherwise.
Multiplication by $2^T$ moves an insertion from $b-1$ into the endpoint
$b$ block. Give each witness $e\in Z_b$ a variable $y_e=2^{u_b+2m+e}$,
used only as the score $-y_e$ in the second stream. There are only three
types for a generator:
\[
 (\eta_g,d_g)\in\{(1,1),(-1,1),(-1,2^T)\}.
\]
The middle type includes early insertions of the chosen parity that are
not valid births. Fix all types, at a cost of at most $3^j$ choices.
Order every block by label. Second-stream generators can be reordered
within their endpoint block because all have mark one; this preserves
pivot marks and endpoint spans, and all retained bases are already counted.

In each stream the absolute scores are distinct powers of two in occurrence
order. Every score dominates the sum of its predecessors. The sign of the
sum over a support therefore equals its last member's mark. For fixed
bases and types these are $2n$ affine functions of the same $j+z$ variables.
Every realized mark pair is a strict sign pattern. The hyperplane-region
bound gives at most $S_{j+z}(2n)$ patterns, proving the claim. Ground-set
size and timeline length affect coefficients, not this dimension bound.
\end{proof}

Allowing all $J,Z$ of size at most $q$ gives a family fixed before the focal
masks with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-family}
 |\mathfrak R|\le192^q S_q(n)^2 S_{2q}(2n).
\end{equation}
The preceding separate count was $4^{3q}S_q(n)^3S_{2q}(n)$; the joint
representation reduces the leading logarithmic entropy coefficient from
five to four, with the new type factor fully included.

Choose an analysis cutoff $\rho<r<1$, and set $q=\lfloor rn/\kappa\rfloor$.
Call $|D\cap U|\le rn$ good. On a good outcome the source generator bound
gives $|J|\le q$. If the nominal rank $z_i\le q$, its witnesses have size
at most $q$ too. If $z_i>q$, then $\kappa z_i>rn\ge|D\cap U|\ge\sigma_i$,
so no residual error is needed. With $\alpha=r/\kappa$, the binomial-sum
bounds, including $q=0$, imply
\[
 \log|\mathfrak R|\le A_r n/\kappa,\qquad
 A_r=r\bigl(4\log(\kappa/r)+4+6\log2+\log3\bigr).
\]
Indeed $\log S_q(n)\le\alpha n\log(e/\alpha)$ and
$\log S_{2q}(2n)\le2\alpha n\log(e/\alpha)$, with floors intact.

Define $e_i$ as the residual size on listed good outcomes with $z_i\le q$,
as $n$ on bad outcomes, and zero otherwise. Pointwise,
$\sigma_i\le\kappa z_i+e_i$. The mask $O=D\cup C$ has independent focal
rate $p_O=\rho+(1-\rho)t$ under the original conditioning. Put
$\lambda=-\log p_O$. The good residual tail is bounded by
$\min(1,|\mathfrak R|e^{-\lambda x})$: drop the good-event intersection
before the union bound, without asserting a conditional product law.
Its integral is at most $(A_rn/\kappa+1)/\lambda$.

Independently, Chernoff's bound gives
\[
 \Prb(|D\cap U|>rn)\le e^{-n d(r\Vert\rho)},\qquad
 d(r\Vert\rho)=r\log\frac r\rho+(1-r)\log\frac{1-r}{1-\rho}.
\]
This follows by Markov's inequality with exponential tilt
$e^s=r(1-\rho)/(\rho(1-r))$ and moment $(1-\rho+\rho e^s)^n$.
Combining the branches, $n\ge\kappa$, and \eqref{eq:safe} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transfer}
 \E[z_i\mid H]\ge\frac{\gamma|Y_i|-\epsilon n_i}{\kappa},\qquad
 \epsilon=\frac{\rho}{2\kappa}+\frac{A_r+1}{\kappa\lambda}
                  +e^{-\kappa d(r\Vert\rho)}.
\end{equation}
The cutoff is only an analysis device and does not restrict the theorem
or add an algorithmic sampling requirement.

\section{Parameters and final accounting}
Choose
\[
 \kappa=640,\quad t=2^{-15},\quad h=\Prb(H_e)=3/4,
 \quad\rho=\Prb(D_e)=7/16,\quad\gamma=7/64.
\]
Guards and tests have rate $t$; parity is fair. Set main-branch probability
$p_{\rm main}=1-t$. The maximum branch retains its separate fair mask.
For rounding take $\theta=261/256$, $L=256$, $B=\theta^L$, and uniform
$J\in\{0,\ldots,L-1\}$, with
\[
 u_J(x)=\theta^J B^{\lfloor\log_B(x/\theta^J)\rfloor},\quad u_J(0)=0.
\]
The source rule otherwise stays the same. All choices are initial and independent.

Take $r=33/64$. Rational atanh-series enclosures with explicit tails give
\[
 A_r+1<41/2,\quad\lambda>413/500,\quad
 \kappa d(r\Vert\rho)>11\log2,\quad B>140.
\]
An upper bound on the error in \eqref{eq:transfer} is therefore
\[
 \bar\epsilon=\frac{7}{32\kappa}+\frac{41/2}{\kappa(413/500)}+\frac1{2048}
 =\frac{335021}{8458240}.
\]

For general filter rate $h$, the elementary source filter argument gives
\[
 \E_H u(Y)=(1-h)W,\quad
 \E_H u(U)=\frac{1-h}{h}\E_H u(\operatorname{greedy}(H))
 \le\frac{1-h}{h}W.
\]
Indeed, condition on preceding memberships in rounded priority: the span
test is fixed, and the probabilities for a candidate and a greedy selected
sample are respectively $1-h$ and $h$ times that test. At $h=3/4$ the two
coefficients are $1/4$ and $1/3$.

Put $\beta=(B-1)/B$ and
\[
 L_0=\bar\epsilon+t\kappa+\frac{t\kappa+\rho}{B-1},\qquad
 s=(1-h)\left(\gamma-\frac{L_0}{h}\right).
\]
The source's order-uniform rank comparison and independent test thinning
give, for large groups,
\[
 \E[\lambda_i\mid H]\ge\frac t\kappa\left[
 \gamma|Y_i|-(\bar\epsilon+t\kappa)n_i
 -(t\kappa+\rho)\sum_{j>i}n_j\right].
\]
The weighted higher-group sum is at most $u(U)/(B-1)$. Omitted groups
$n_i<\kappa$ cost at most $\kappa m_*/\beta$ in comparison mass, where
$m_*$ is the maximum rounded nonloop weight. Consequently
\[
 \E_R\min_\pi\mathrm{reward}_{\rm main}
 \ge\frac{\beta t s}{\kappa}W-t\gamma m_*.
\]
The maximum branch guarantees $m_*/4$ in worst-order expectation, and
$(1-p_{\rm main})/4=t/4\ge p_{\rm main}t\gamma$, cancelling the negative term.

For a positive $x$, let $k=\lfloor\log_\theta x\rfloor$. Its rounded values
over all phases are $\theta^{k-r}$ for $r=0,\ldots,L-1$, once each.
Since $\theta^k\ge x/\theta$, the geometric sum gives
\[
 \E_Ju_J(x)\ge\frac{\beta}{L(\theta-1)}x=\frac\beta5x.
\]
Apply this to a fixed true optimal independent set to obtain the same
factor for expected rounded optimum. Thus the final coefficient is
\[
 C=\frac{p_{\rm main}\beta^2 t s}{5\kappa}.
\]
Exact integer arithmetic gives $261^{256}>140\cdot256^{256}$, hence
$\beta>139/140$ and
\[
 s>\frac\gamma4-\frac13\left(\bar\epsilon+t\kappa+
 \frac{t\kappa+\rho}{139}\right)
 =\frac{23047361}{3527086080}>0.
\]
Substitution gives $C>C_1>(21/20)2^{-34}$ as stated. Every scalar comparison
is certified with exact rational arithmetic. Relative to the published
\[
C_0=\frac{9652683394380719}{637507023635152349901619200},
\]
the conservative certificate satisfies $C_1/C_0=4.056975824\ldots>4$.

The minimum over all permutations remains inside the seed expectation at
every stage. The source's glued-vector simulation therefore transfers this
coefficient losslessly to one sample per label. Conditional on the entire
seed the glued vector has the product value law; ignored samples, sacrificed
values, and unused coins may all still inform the adversary's order.

\section{Resources and evidence}
Per label draw $2+4+15+15+1=37$ fair bits for the filter, density mask,
guard, test, and maximum mask. Shared bits are $8+15+1=24$ for the phase,
branch, and parity, including unused-branch randomness.
All new scalar constants and phase multipliers are fixed rationals.
Rank/closure tables and flat enumeration may be exponential and remain
charged preprocessing and storage. Observation precision and integer level
indices are unbounded in the original exact-real measurable model. Rational
input implementations can compare exact rational powers, with costs depending
on bit lengths and exponents. No polynomial complexity is claimed.

The local exact checks cover 4,916 reverse-process quadratic resource and
drift inequalities and 3,153 global inequalities on 170 small matroid/weight
instances. Additional checks cover all 199 increasing Boolean events on at
most four bits at two rates, 5,992 biased-filter instances, 560 selected-residual
expectations, and 40 rational-rounding boundary cases. The abstract sharpness
formula is checked by enumeration. These are finite evidence; the universal
claim rests on the written argument and cited source dependencies.

New exact checks cover 45,824 residual encodings and 331,491 affine pivot
signs over 170 matroid/weight instances, including early invalid births and
shifted generators. They also cover 48 binomial tails, 120 floor/binomial-sum
cases, and 24 new-phase boundaries. An explicit negative control rejects
omitting the score multiplier. These finite checks do not establish the
uniform joint-counting lemma.

The same-agent audit rechecked the query martingale, predictable depleted
probability, both terminal outcomes, frozen-state containment, telescope,
integrated tail at $n=\kappa$, changed mask factors, rounding, and simulation.
The new same-agent pass also checked shared variables, all three types, fixed
old scores, the arrangement dimension, and the cutoff conditioning.
Independent review and formalization remain outstanding. Repository
preparation does not constitute independent validation, a priority
determination, or a confirmed best-known-bound claim.
